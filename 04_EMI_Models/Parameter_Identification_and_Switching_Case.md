## What the Phase 2B reduced-order model can identify

The implemented `physical_coupling_profile.m` uses:

- Capacitive disturbance scale: `alphaC * Rdiff * (Cplus - Cminus) * deltaV / tr * Hrx`.
- Inductive disturbance scale: `alphaM * (Mplus - Mminus) * deltaI / ti * Hrx`.
- Shared-return disturbance scale: `kCMtoDM * (Rreturn * deltaI + Lreturn * deltaI / ti) * Hrx`.
- Ground contribution: `kGround * Vground`.
- Equivalent angular error: `Ksys * Vreceiver`.

These equations imply structural confounding: output amplitude alone cannot distinguish a larger coupling imbalance from a larger path-transfer factor, a faster source edge, or a larger voltage-to-angle gain. Only the capacitance and mutual-inductance differences enter the present model; equal increases to both line values have no effect. The same closed-loop record cannot independently identify all these quantities.

Receiver bandwidth is applied at the assumed 120 Hz envelope frequency, so changing a megahertz bandwidth has almost no effect. Differential capacitance supplies a reported termination pole but does not filter the waveform. PWM frequency, voltage fall time, minimum pulse width, and the count cap do not currently enter the generated disturbance waveform. A negligible sensitivity for these inputs is a statement about this implementation, not evidence that the physical quantities are unimportant.

The model's 0.20 V diagnostic compares noise alone with an assumed margin. A physical receiver switches on the total intended signal plus disturbance, subject to common-mode conditions, hysteresis and timing. TI's RS-422 discussion distinguishes differential input sensitivity from common-mode operation; those limits do not establish a universal 0.20 V noise-to-false-count rule. See [TI, RS-422 and RS-485 Standards Overview and System Configurations](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slla070d/slla070d.pdf).

## Circuit specification: SC-01, one switching aggressor and one differential victim pair

### Purpose and scope

Resolve how a finite switching edge, explicit coupling/return paths and a loaded receiver input produce differential and common-mode transients. The first deliverable is a short electrical transient harness with recorded threshold-exposure events. It does not yet simulate a full robot, a full encoder decoder, communication frames or electromagnetic compliance.

Use two implementation stages within this case:

1. **SC-01A: prescribed finite-edge source harness.** Use controlled electrical voltage/current sources with declared finite ramp durations to exercise passive coupling paths independently. This permits equation and sign checks before device data exist. These are test excitations; voltage and current are not claimed to come from a physically selected motor drive.
2. **SC-01B: device switching source.** Replace the prescribed source with a single 24 V half-bridge and inductive-load commutation cell after a MOSFET/driver/diode model and required parasitic data are recorded. Include gate drive impedance, dead time, charge/capacitance effects, DC-link impedance and relevant loop parasitics. Match edge shape over held-out operating points before accepting the source replacement.

MathWorks separates ideal switching models without charge dynamics from models suitable for device-scale transients, and notes the additional parameterization needed for charge models. That supports staging the source fidelity according to the question being tested. See [Choose Blocks to Model Semiconductor Devices](https://www.mathworks.com/help/sps/ug/choose-right-semiconductor-block.html). SC-01A remains useful even when SC-01B lacks the necessary component data.

**Current definition:** the assumed 3 A change in 200 ns must refer to a commutation branch. Applying it to the existing 2.5 mH motor inductance would imply `L*di/dt = 37,500 V`. In SC-01B the winding current follows the circuit equations while switch/diode branches commutate; the source-current label must identify the measured branch.

### Circuit construction and signals

- Put separate capacitors from the aggressor node to each victim conductor; include their reference, shield and load connections explicitly. Begin with the inherited 10 pF/9 pF values labeled assumed.
- Put passive mutual coupling between the identified aggressor/victim loops only after self-inductances and sign conventions are supplied. A controlled `M*di/dt` source is acceptable for an isolated equation check, but does not establish reciprocal circuit behavior.
- Put the inherited 25 milliohm + 20 nH shared return at the actual common path. Model common-mode conversion through explicit asymmetries, or retain one clearly identified behavioral transfer for an isolated check. Do not count both as independent versions of the same path.
- Load the victim with the 120 ohm differential termination and 100 pF differential capacitance as explicit circuit elements. Give the encoder-side source a declared impedance. The 12 ns standalone `R*C` product is only an initial time-scale estimate; the full network and source impedance determine the actual poles.
- Include intended victim logic levels and switching phase as declared test stimuli. Log `Vdiff = Vplus - Vminus` and `Vcm = (Vplus + Vminus)/2` relative to receiver ground. Initially report threshold-exposure duration and crossing times; add receiver hysteresis/propagation/pulse behavior when supported by its model. One victim pair cannot predict complete quadrature count errors.
- Keep the lumped approximation conditional on propagation delay relative to the fastest relevant edges. Add a distributed or segmented line when cable delay/reflections matter, and converge segment count. TI discusses termination and delay/edge-time considerations in [SLLA070D](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slla070d/slla070d.pdf); MathWorks provides available fidelity choices in [Choose Blocks to Model Cables or Lines](https://www.mathworks.com/help/sps/ug/choose-cable-or-line-block.html).

Start with a single edge and both polarities in a 5 microsecond record. For repetition effects, use 10 periods at the inherited 20 kHz (0.5 ms), with a declared 50% duty as a test condition, and compare with 20 periods. Check fixed victim levels and several victim-transition phases. Add isolated capacitive, inductive and shared-return cases, a zero-coupling reference, balanced-pair checks, then combined coupling. This is a finite deterministic matrix, not a statistical reliability prediction.

## Numerical convergence and acceptance criteria

These project acceptance criteria were applied to SC-01A; they are not limits supplied by a manufacturer or an EMC standard. The implementation uses the refined peak/area for the 1% comparison, a 0.1 mV voltage floor and a 1e-11 V*s area floor (0.1 mV times the 100 ns ramp).

1. Start with a variable-step Simscape solver and continuous/event-resolved logging. MathWorks recommends a variable-step baseline before fixed-step optimization; see [Making Optimal Solver Choices for Physical Simulation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simscape/ug/making-optimal-solver-choices-for-physical-simulation.html). Record the solver, release, tolerances, maximum step and model revision.
2. For the initial assumed network, begin with a 1 ns maximum step, relative tolerance `1e-5`, and state scaling/absolute tolerances chosen so receiver-voltage numerical noise is well below 0.1 mV. Reduce the maximum step to 0.5 ns and then 0.25 ns, and tighten tolerances tenfold on representative cases. If faster poles are introduced, revise the starting step. A nanosecond logging interval alone does not constrain integration accuracy.
3. Require successive refinements to change differential/common-mode peak voltage and integrated disturbance by no more than 1%, using a 0.1 mV absolute floor for near-zero voltage comparisons. Require exposure duration and crossing-time changes below 1 ns for crossings sufficiently separated from tangency. Classify threshold-grazing cases as numerically unresolved until additional refinement establishes stability; do not quietly count them as passes.
4. Check single-path analytical limits with their explicit loading assumptions: `i = C*dv/dt`, `v = M*di/dt`, and `vreturn = R*i + L*di/dt`. Check polarity reversal, zero source/coupling and balanced-pair behavior. The explicit loaded network need not reproduce the old scalar path factors unless it is deliberately configured to represent them.
5. For a passive linear network, verify superposition within numerical tolerance before adding nonlinear receiver clamps. With nonlinear elements enabled, inspect residuals/energy balance and record legitimate departure from superposition.
6. Compare the repeated-pulse metrics between the 10- and 20-period records; extend if ringing, bias or stored energy has not settled. If a segmented cable is used, double segment count and apply the same waveform criteria. Record runtime so a faster configuration can be justified against the converged result.

Save the circuit schematic/model, complete input manifest with provenance, raw time-domain voltage/current data, overlay plots from refinements, convergence table, threshold-event records and a validity statement. Completion of SC-01 establishes a tested circuit-model workflow. Agreement with independently measured waveforms is a later physical-validation result and must be reported separately.

## SC-01A completion record, 2026-09-09

The implementation and saved results are in `06_Circuit_Simulations/SC01A`. All 67 project tests pass. The 16-case, 51-run native campaign passes exact-reference comparisons, both step refinements per case, three tighter-tolerance checks, fixed-network superposition/polarity/symmetry checks and the 10-versus-20-period settling comparison. Full refined native traces, complete case/parameter manifests, threshold events, refinement overlays and the runnable SLX are retained.

The circuit uses actual capacitive branches and a loaded shared return, plus explicitly behavioral inductive sources. The loaded nominal receiver differential is approximately 1.085917 V for a +2 V open-circuit source; the combined rising-edge disturbance peaks near 51.7 mV. The asymmetric stress case is exploratory. See the generated validation summary for exact results and limitations. The earlier Phase 2B equations and parameter-identification discussion above are retained as provenance; they are not the new circuit equations.

## SC-01B implementation record, 2026-09-09

`06_Circuit_Simulations/SC01B` implements an isothermal IAUC100N04S6L014 half-bridge with nonlinear gate/output charge, body diode and package parasitics from the shipped vendor model. A declared UCC27211A-informed behavioral drive supplies gate commands; the full driver IC and bootstrap dynamics are omitted. Supply/decoupling and load parameters remain assumptions. The commutation current follows the circuit rather than an imposed current slope.

The frozen study retains five operating points, physical integration-step refinements, independent original-SPICE waveforms, event records, internal native channel/diode currents and external energy closure. The 47 ohm gate-resistance point is a documented failed stress case: slow gate behavior permits simultaneous channel conduction. Numerical agreement on this failure is not an acceptable gate-drive design. Exact results and numerical limits live in the generated SC-01B report; these source gates are distinct from the SC-01A receiver-voltage gates above.

Next evidence priorities are a characterized gate-drive mitigation, measured commutation waveforms and layout/loop impedance, followed by source-to-coupling integration with derivative/transfer sensitivity checks. Receiver decoding, temperature dependence and physical source validation remain open.
